How AiMY Sales works for each of its five users, process by process. Every process links to the live prototype and shows what you should see.

Before you start

  • Each process has an Open button. It opens the prototype as the right person, on the right page.
  • Start each walk-through fresh. The prototype remembers what you change, in your browser only. To start over, press the small sliders button at the bottom right of the prototype, then Reset to seed.
  • Dates move with the calendar. A callback that is "due yesterday" will still be due yesterday when you open it next month.
  • Screenshots are from 26 October 2026, at a 1536px wide screen. Click a screenshot to open it full size; click the small pictures under it to see the next step.

The words we use

Use these words in designs, tickets and conversations, the same way the product uses them.

Desk
One person's view of the product. There are five, one per role.
Contacts
The people we approach. For a caller this is who to call; for a manager it is their deals.
Account
A company. A company that already buys from us is a customer.
Campaign
A push to reach a goal, such as "4 new clients", in one market, selling one or more of our services. A manager owns it and callers work it.
List
A set of people found for a market. Lists feed campaigns; every campaign has at least one.
The ladder
Where a lead stands with the caller, from Not called to Handed over.
Deal stage
Where a lead stands with the manager after the hand-over.
AiMY
The assistant. You type or speak to it in the bar at the bottom of every page. It answers in a card above the bar, and opens full screen for longer work.
Brief
What AiMY tells you before a call or a meeting: how to open, what happened last time, what they may push back on.
Bell
Notifications and follow-ups, each with one action.

Two wording rules: the verb is always "call", never "ring", and copy uses plain English with no sales slang.

The ladder

Not called→No answer→Callback→Answered→Meeting set→Showed up→Interested→Handed over

A lead can also leave the ladder: Declined, Wrong number or Do not call. A call never moves a lead backwards. Every move can be undone.

Deal stages

Not met→Scoped→Shown→Priced→WonLostFollow-up

How a call can end

Seven outcomes, each on a number key: 1 Connected, 2 Callback, 3 No answer, 4 Gatekeeper, 5 Not interested, 6 Wrong number, 7 Do not call.

Who can do what

  • Make calls: the caller and the sales manager.
  • Move a lead or a deal: the caller moves the ladder; the manager moves deal stages.
  • Build and run a campaign: the manager. The stakeholder and client request one; the CEO writes one and gives it to a manager.
  • Find leads: everyone except the client.
  • Add a lead: everyone. A lead from the stakeholder or client goes straight to a manager.
  • Join a deal: the CEO, and the stakeholder on his own product.
  • See costs and how we rate accounts: everyone except the client.

Around the screen

Every desk shares the same frame, which AiMY Sales shares with AiMY Knowledge and AiMY QA.

  • Top bar: the AiMY product switcher, the phone (caller only), light or dark theme, the bell, and the person's name.
  • Left column: what needs you now, today's diary, and the money or the client's report.
  • Page title: the title is also the tab switcher (Contacts, Diary, Campaigns, Lists). Each tab shows how many are in it.
  • The AiMY bar: type a question, a name, or a sentence such as "Spoke to Amelia, she wants a demo". AiMY answers in the card above. The microphone dictates; the round button starts a voice conversation.
  • Undo, not "are you sure": every change shows a short message with Undo.
  • Back links return to exactly where you were before.
  • Prototype panel (sliders button, bottom right): make someone call in, show different states, reset.

B1Work the queue

Goal: pick who to call next.
OpenOpen the After meeting filter
  1. Read the Today briefing. Each underlined phrase opens the set it names.
  2. Use the Start strip: Call the next one, Work the callbacks, Say what happened, Find leads.
  3. Under Contacts, pick a filter: All, Callbacks, New, No answer, Answered, After meeting. Each filter is a step on the ladder, so they add up to All.
  4. Each card shows the step, the campaign, who they are, AiMY's reason they are here, and the number. Press Call. Fifteen cards per page; the pager is at the bottom.
  5. On After meeting, each card asks "They showed up" or "Did not show", one press each.

You should see: 109 to call; Callbacks 5, New 69, No answer 21, Answered 14, After meeting 4. Amelia Taylor is first.

B2Make a call and log it

Goal: one call, from ready to logged. The call is simulated: a transcript grows a line at a time.
Open Daisy Watson
  1. Press Call on Daisy Watson. The call panel opens on the right, ready to dial, and AiMY's brief opens: Open with, Last time, If they push back, Selling, Asking for, You can send.
  2. Optional: choose which of our numbers to call from with the From menu. AiMY reuses the number this person has seen before.
  3. Press the blue Call from button. It shows Connecting for 1.6 seconds, then live with a timer.
  4. Press the record button and agree to the consent question. The transcript only grows while recording.
  5. Press the red hang-up button. AiMY reads the call back: "I read that as connected. Is that right?", with outcome, what they asked for, the new ladder step and the transcript.
  6. Correct it by typing, or press 1 to 7. Then Log it, or Log it and call <next>.
  7. A message confirms the log and the ladder move, with Undo.

Keyboard: Enter always does the obvious next thing (call, place, hang up, log). 1 to 7 pick an outcome. S skips. J/K move between cards, O opens one. Esc closes the innermost thing.

B3Call a whole page in one run

Goal: call everyone on a campaign or list page, with a wait between calls (Jira AIMY-2667).
Open campaign c4Open list ls2
  1. On a campaign page press Call all 15 at the end of the filter row. On a list page it sits in the "Who is on it" header.
  2. The first call opens in ready. Place it, then log it as usual.
  3. The next call opens with a countdown and a draining bar. At zero it places itself. Call now skips the wait.
  4. The brief carries Skip this one and Stop the run. At the end AiMY says "That run is finished".

Where the wait comes from: default 30 seconds, 5 to 120 in steps of 5. A manager sets it on the campaign (create or Edit). A list on no campaign has its own stepper. A list on a campaign shows a locked pill: the campaign's wait wins.

UX decisions

The decisions that shaped AiMY Sales, and why each was made. Keep them unless the reason no longer holds.

How the product is organised

Open on a briefing, not a database

Every desk opens on "Today": a few sentences on what needs you, then a Start strip of the next things to do.

Why: leaders rejected the earlier table-first version. People come to the product to know what to do next, not to browse records.

One product, five desks

The caller, manager, stakeholder, client and CEO use the same screens. The more senior the person, the more they see and the less they change.

Why: one set of words and one set of numbers. A manager and the CEO can never read two different figures for the same thing.

The page title is the tab switcher

Contacts, Diary, Campaigns and Lists sit in the heading itself, with a count on each. There is no navigation column.

Why: a product with four places to go does not need a row of menus to say which one you are in.

Fifteen at a time, not an endless list

Lists show fifteen cards and a button for the next fifteen, with "1 to 15 of 109" written underneath.

Why: with a thousand rows behind a scrollbar you cannot tell where you are, come back to the same place, or ever finish.

How work gets done

Logging a call is confirming, not filling a form

When a call ends AiMY says what it heard ("I read that as connected. Is that right?"). One press logs it, or one sentence corrects it.

Why: the call already holds the answer. A form after the call asks the caller to report the same call twice, 200 times a day.

The call sits beside the page

A call opens in a panel on the right, never in a pop-up window.

Why: during a call you need the person's record and the campaign's pitch. A pop-up would cover the very things you need.

Undo instead of "are you sure?"

Every change happens on one click and shows a short message with Undo. There are no confirmation dialogs or pop-up forms.

Why: dialogs slow down every action to protect against the rare mistake. Undo protects against the mistake and costs nothing the rest of the time.

Say it in a sentence

The AiMY bar takes plain sentences: "Had a demo with Noah, they want a proposal next week" moves the deal and books the follow-up.

Why: managers are on the road and wrote meeting notes on paper. A sentence, typed or spoken, is the fastest way to keep the record true.

Finding leads is a conversation

There is no search builder page. You describe who you want; AiMY finds them and brings back a list to review, name and save.

Why: people know who they want in words, not in filters. The review step keeps the person in charge of what gets saved.

What a person saw is one press

"They showed up" and "They are interested" are buttons on the contact, not something AiMY works out.

Why: no call record can prove that someone turned up or is interested. Only the person who was there knows.

Quick settings change in place, longer text behind Edit

A team or the wait between calls changes right on the page. A pitch or notes are changed through Edit.

Why: the line is whether a change has a middle. A setting is done in one click; a paragraph is a draft until you finish it.

The campaign sets the pace of calls

The wait between calls is set on the campaign and wins over any list's own wait. During a run the caller sees only the countdown and "Call now".

Why: one owner for the pace, and nothing for the caller to fiddle with in the middle of a run.

Who sees what

The client sees results, not our operation

The client never sees our costs, how we rate accounts, or the names of the people we call. Her report counts promises kept, by calendar quarter.

Why: she bought an outcome. How many times we called someone is our work, not her delivery, and a quarter gives her a regular point to check progress, instead of one figure at the end of the year.

A client's request is talked through first

When a client asks for a campaign, her account manager books a meeting with her before it can run.

Why: a campaign spends real money and calls on her behalf. Agreeing the details face to face avoids running the wrong one.

The CEO assigns; managers run

The CEO reads everything and changes nothing. He gives work to a manager, with AiMY's suggestion and its reason, and joins deals that need him.

Why: the work stays with the people accountable for it, and the reason travels with the assignment so the manager knows why it is theirs.

Follow-ups come back in the bell

When work leaves a desk (a hand-over, an assignment, a request) its news returns to the sender's bell.

Why: a review of all five desks found the same gap everywhere: work that left a desk never came back. Today stays about today.

A page belongs to whoever does the work

Scoring individual support agents lives in AiMY QA. The client report links there rather than copying it.

Why: one place for each job. Two copies drift apart and then disagree.

Only public facts about a person

A contact's profile shows their job, career, public posts and how to reach them. No guesses about team size or who they report to.

Why: callers act on these facts. A guess presented as a fact loses trust the first time it is wrong.

How it looks and reads

Plain words, one word per thing

"Call", never "ring". "Contacts" for the caller and the manager alike. No sales slang.

Why: a caller handing a lead to a manager should not have to translate. Plain words also read the same to a client.

No faded text

A state is shown in words, weight and colour, never by greying text out.

Why: faded text is hard to read, and what is hard to read gets missed.

Change shape, never hide

On a small screen the left column becomes a drawer and long rows wrap. Nothing is removed.

Why: hiding a control on a small screen takes away something the person can do.

Show the one thing that matters, expand for the rest

A company's news shows its single best opening for us, with the rest opening in place underneath.

Why: there are at most a few openings worth acting on. A side panel would cover the page and clash with the call panel.
